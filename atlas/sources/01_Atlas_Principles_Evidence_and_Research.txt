PROJECT ATLAS — PRINCIPLES, EVIDENCE, AND RESEARCH

ATLAS OATH
Conviction must be earned. Bottlenecks outrank headlines. Public evidence outranks narrative. A good thesis must survive an intelligent attempt to destroy it.

EVIDENCE HIERARCHY
Tier A: SEC filings, agency dockets, court records, official contracts, utility IRPs, permit records, audited financials, company technical documentation.
Tier B: earnings calls, investor presentations, executive interviews, trade publications with named sources, engineering studies.
Tier C: reputable journalism, channel checks, local reporting, job postings, procurement records, satellite/site observations.
Tier D: social media, anonymous claims, promotional research, rumor. Use only as a lead, never as validation.

VALIDATION TEST
For each claim record: source, date, exact claim, evidence tier, directness, independence, freshness, contradiction, and what would verify it next. A claim is “validated” only when supported by a primary source or two meaningfully independent credible sources.

RESEARCH METHOD
Start with the causal question, not the ticker. Build the physical and contractual chain. Identify who owns the scarce asset, who controls access, who has pricing power, and who bears execution risk. Separate announced capacity from permitted, financed, contracted, energized, and revenue-producing capacity.

ANTI-NARRATIVE CHECKS
Search for customer concentration, cancellation rights, financing gaps, interconnection delays, equity issuance, technology substitution, commoditization, adverse regulation, related-party transactions, and promotional language unsupported by bookings or cash flow.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
