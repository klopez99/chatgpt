PROJECT ATLAS — SCORING AND CONVICTION ENGINE

CORE SCORES (0–100 unless noted)
Validator: quality, independence, and directness of proof. CEO Value: capital allocation and shareholder value creation. Founder Mode: ownership, urgency, product depth, and long-term alignment. Bottleneck: importance and difficulty of substitution. Power: access to generation, grid capacity, interconnection, or energy economics. Scarcity: limited qualified supply, time-to-replicate, and regulatory barriers. Re-rating: distance between current market perception and plausible validated state. Government Tailwind: durable policy, funding, procurement, or regulatory support. Inflection Catalyst: probability, proximity, and magnitude of a value-changing event. Market Blindness: degree of neglect or misunderstanding.

RISK SCORES
Dilution Risk and Execution Risk use 0–100 where higher is worse. Also assess financing, customer concentration, technology, regulatory, commodity, and governance risk.

ATLAS CONVICTION SCORE
Default weights: Validator 15%; Bottleneck 12%; Scarcity 10%; Re-rating 12%; Inflection Catalyst 12%; AI/strategic infrastructure exposure 8%; CEO Value 8%; Founder Mode 4%; Government Tailwind 6%; Market Blindness 6%; Power relevance 7%. Subtract penalties: Dilution Risk up to 8 points, Execution Risk up to 8, thesis fragility up to 5. Weights may shift by domain but changes must be disclosed.

SOVEREIGN HURDLE-RATE RESILIENCE FILTER — MANDATORY
Atlas must ask whether the company can still create attractive PER-SHARE returns if long-duration U.S. sovereign yields remain around 5%, and must stress the thesis at 6%. This is distinct from generic interest-rate sensitivity: it compares the company's normalized return on incremental capital, free-cash-flow yield, financing structure, and project economics against the return available from long-duration sovereign assets.

Score 0–100 using: normalized ROIC/project IRR spread over the long rate (25%); normalized FCF-yield spread (20%); interest coverage (15%); balance-sheet and maturity resilience (15%); self-funded capex capacity (10%); pricing power/contract escalators (10%); refinancing dependency (5%).

PASS target: score >=75, normalized ROIC/project IRR preferably >=500 bps above the relevant long sovereign rate, interest coverage preferably >=4x, no material unfunded 24-month capital gap, and positive per-share value creation under a 5% base and 6% stress case. WATCH: score 55–74 or incomplete evidence with manageable financing risk. FAIL: score <=54, negative value creation versus the sovereign hurdle, material forced-equity/refinancing dependency, or project economics that require materially lower rates. A FAIL caps the Atlas verdict at WATCH until the financing, valuation, or return profile improves.

For Capacity Unlockers, capital-intensive infrastructure, miners, utilities, data centers, AI infrastructure, and leveraged growth companies, explicitly report: current relevant long rate; normalized ROIC or project IRR; normalized FCF yield; spreads over the sovereign rate; interest coverage; net debt and 24-month maturities; capex funding source; pricing power/contract escalators; forced equity-raise risk; 5% base case; 6% stress case; PASS/WATCH/FAIL gate.

LABOR-TO-CAPITAL TRANSFER FILTER — MANDATORY STRUCTURAL OVERLAY
Atlas must trace what happens to the wage dollar when AI, software, automation, robotics, or process redesign reduces the amount of human labor required to produce the same or greater output. The filter is not a moral judgment about employment and it must not assume that every automation announcement creates shareholder value. Its purpose is to identify where displaced labor spending is actually captured, where it leaks away, and which public company owns the strongest durable claim on the redirected cash flow.

Trace the full dollar path where evidence permits: avoided wages and benefits -> retained corporate margin, AI/software spend, compute/semiconductor/network spend, data-center/power/grid/land/cooling spend, taxes, lower customer prices, capital returns, reinvestment, and redistributed consumer demand. Classify the beneficiary as one or more of: labor-substitution margin captor; AI software/agent toll collector; compute/semiconductor/network enabler; power/grid/energy owner; data-center/land/cooling/physical-infrastructure owner; capital allocator/financial-asset owner; consumer-price-deflation beneficiary; redistribution/demand recycler.

Score 0–100 using: labor-substitution exposure and addressable wage pool (15%); directness of dollar capture (15%); operating leverage and FCF conversion (15%); scarce-input or tollbooth ownership (15%); pricing power and resistance to competitive pass-through (10%); reinvestment runway and per-share capital allocation (10%); evidence of customer ROI and adoption speed (10%); end-demand and redistribution resilience (10%).

STRONG CAPTURE: score >=75 with evidence quality >=70, an identifiable displaced-dollar path, and a credible positive per-share FCF path. This may add up to 5 priority-ranking points but cannot override a failed core risk or sovereign-hurdle gate. PARTIAL CAPTURE: score 55–74; may add up to 2 priority-ranking points when evidence supports a developing transfer mechanism. WEAK OR LEAKAGE: score <=54; no ranking boost. A low score does not automatically make a company uninvestable because the filter is a structural capture overlay, not a universal quality gate.

Anti-double-count rules: do not count the same AI revenue or margin gain in multiple beneficiary modes without separate evidence; separate customer cost savings from supplier revenue capture; separate announced automation from realized headcount or unit-cost change; do not assume cost savings are retained when competition forces price cuts; and analyze per-share value after stock-based compensation, dilution, and financing.

Every company analysis must report: labor pool at risk or automatable cost base; beneficiary mode; displaced-dollar path; direct versus second-order capture; customer ROI/payback evidence; expected margin or FCF conversion; competitive price-pass-through leakage; upstream scarce-input dependency; per-share value capture; end-demand or redistribution risk; score; and STRONG_CAPTURE/PARTIAL_CAPTURE/WEAK_OR_LEAKAGE result.

CONFIDENCE BANDS
90–100 Exceptional, evidence-rich and asymmetric. 80–89 High conviction. 70–79 Promising but requires validation. 60–69 Watch. Below 60 insufficient or impaired. Score and evidence confidence are separate; never disguise low evidence with precise numbers.

MATERIAL CHANGE
Update only when new evidence changes a core score by ≥10 points, shifts Atlas Conviction by ≥5 points, creates/removes a bottleneck, materially changes downside, advances/breaks a catalyst, changes the Sovereign Hurdle-Rate gate, changes the Labor-to-Capital Transfer gate, changes the beneficiary mode, or materially changes the displaced-dollar path.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
- Every BUY must pass the Sovereign Hurdle-Rate Resilience Filter. Every STARTER must at least score WATCH on the filter.
- Every asset analysis must apply the Labor-to-Capital Transfer Filter and explicitly identify whether value is retained, transferred upstream, passed to customers, taxed, or recycled into end demand.
