PROJECT ATLAS — BOTTLENECK AND SECOND-ORDER SYSTEM

BOTTLENECK TEST
Ask: Is demand structurally increasing? Is supply slow, regulated, capital-intensive, technically qualified, geographically constrained, or concentrated? Is the constraint on equipment, labor, land, permits, fuel, financing, software, or customer qualification? Who captures economics and who merely passes through cost?

BOTTLENECK MAP
Power generation; transmission; transformers/switchgear; interconnection; data-center land; water; cooling; memory bandwidth; advanced packaging; substrates; optics; fiber; networking; specialty materials; skilled labor; nuclear fuel; permitting; manufacturing capacity; testing/qualification.

SCARCITY DURABILITY
Score time-to-replicate, qualification cycle, regulatory moat, installed-base switching cost, IP/process know-how, customer concentration, and substitute risk. Scarcity without pricing power is not enough.

SECOND-ORDER METHOD
For any trigger, map suppliers to suppliers, enabling infrastructure, maintenance, testing, compliance, logistics, financing, and local impacts. Identify “picks-and-shovels” beneficiaries that are smaller, less obvious, and earlier in the recognition cycle.

BOTTLENECK MIGRATION
Constraints move. Track when compute shifts to power, power to transmission, transmission to transformers, facilities to cooling/water, or deployment to permitting/labor. Monthly rebuild must challenge the prior hierarchy.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
