PROJECT ATLAS — DATA CENTERS, LAND, PERMITTING, AND WATER

CAPACITY LADDER
Concept → land option → controlled site → zoning → environmental review → water/sewer approval → utility study → interconnection agreement → financing → construction → commissioned capacity → contracted/occupied → revenue. Never equate announced MW with operational MW.

SITE QUALITY
Power availability and price; fiber routes; latency; water/cooling; natural hazards; tax incentives; labor; community acceptance; land control; expansion rights; title/environmental issues.

PERMITTING ENGINE
Track planning commissions, county agendas, building permits, environmental filings, air permits, water allocations, road improvements, tax agreements, and litigation. Local records often precede national coverage.

BUSINESS MODELS
Hyperscale owner, colocation, powered shell, build-to-suit, landlord, energy-to-compute conversion, and speculative developer. Evaluate who funds construction and who bears lease-up risk.

RED FLAGS
Site acquisition without power, vague “AI-ready” language, water constraints, community moratoria, unpriced utility upgrades, customer concentration, and lease economics omitted.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
