PROJECT ATLAS — COOLING, THERMAL, AND WATER

SYSTEM MAP
Chip/package thermal interface → cold plate/immersion → coolant distribution → pumps/heat exchangers → chillers/dry coolers/cooling towers → facility controls → water treatment/reuse.

DRIVERS
Rack density, accelerator power, climate, water availability, facility retrofit constraints, energy efficiency, uptime, and customer qualification.

BOTTLENECKS
Qualified liquid-cooling components, leak-free manifolds, pumps, heat exchangers, controls, installation labor, refrigerants, and local water/air permits.

VALIDATION
Named customer qualification, production capacity, backlog, design wins, deployment at scale, warranty/reliability data, and margin economics. A prototype is not a scaled solution.

SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS
Water treatment, industrial pumps, heat reuse, thermal materials, facility engineering, sensors, and maintenance.

RISK
Rapid standard changes, hyperscaler insourcing, commoditization, warranty liabilities, and capacity expansion ahead of demand.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
