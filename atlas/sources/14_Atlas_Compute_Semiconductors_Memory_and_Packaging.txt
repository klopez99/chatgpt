PROJECT ATLAS — COMPUTE, SEMICONDUCTORS, MEMORY, AND PACKAGING

STACK
Accelerators/CPUs → HBM/DRAM/NAND → advanced packaging/interposers/substrates → power delivery → test/burn-in → servers/racks → software utilization.

BOTTLENECKS
HBM bandwidth and yields, CoWoS/advanced packaging, substrates, test capacity, power delivery, optical I/O, specialty materials, and memory-controller/IP constraints.

MEMORY TEST
Determine whether the constraint is bits, bandwidth, latency, packaging, controller/IP, or customer qualification. Track supply additions and pricing cycles because scarcity can reverse quickly.

VALIDATION
Foundry and memory capex, customer qualification, tool orders, capacity utilization, lead times, export rules, technical benchmarks, and revenue concentration.

PUBLIC-MARKET ANGLES
Equipment, materials, test, thermal, memory IP, packaging, substrates, power semiconductors, and specialty manufacturing—especially smaller suppliers whose revenue inflects after qualification.

RISK
Cyclic overbuild, customer insourcing, node transitions, export restrictions, technological substitution, and capex timing.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
