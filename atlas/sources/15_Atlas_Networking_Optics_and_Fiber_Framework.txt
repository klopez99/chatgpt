PROJECT ATLAS — NETWORKING, OPTICS, AND FIBER

STACK
NIC/DPU → switch silicon → switches/routers → copper/active electrical cables → transceivers → lasers/modulators/DSP → fiber → metro/long-haul/interconnect.

DRIVERS
Scale-up versus scale-out traffic, cluster size, training/inference mix, latency, power per bit, and data-center campus geography.

BOTTLENECKS
800G/1.6T qualification, optical components, laser capacity, packaging, DSP, fiber routes, installation, and network operating expertise.

VALIDATION
Customer design wins, production qualification, shipments rather than samples, yield, capacity expansion, lead times, interoperability, and margin capture.

SECOND-ORDER EFFECTS
Fiber construction, test/measurement, connector cleaning, network timing, power management, and optical packaging.

RISK
Rapid price erosion, standards transitions, customer concentration, inventory corrections, Chinese competition, and hyperscaler custom designs.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
