PROJECT ATLAS — DEFENSE, AEROSPACE, AND GOVERNMENT

SIGNAL SOURCES
Federal procurement databases, budget documents, appropriations, agency awards, OTA contracts, IDIQs, program offices, export approvals, congressional testimony, and contractor filings.

CONTRACT QUALITY
Differentiate ceiling from funded value, prime from subcontractor, prototype from program of record, cost-plus from fixed-price, and backlog from options. Estimate revenue timing and margin.

TAILWIND SCORE
Policy durability, bipartisan support, strategic necessity, funding visibility, procurement urgency, domestic-content requirements, and allied demand.

BOTTLENECKS
Qualified suppliers, energetics, propulsion, radiation-hardened electronics, secure communications, shipyard capacity, aerospace forgings, rare materials, and cleared labor.

RISK
Budget delays, protest, fixed-price losses, export controls, security restrictions, customer concentration, and program cancellation. Government interest without appropriated funding is not validation.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
