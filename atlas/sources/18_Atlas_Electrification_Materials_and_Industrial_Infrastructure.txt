PROJECT ATLAS — ELECTRIFICATION, MATERIALS, AND INDUSTRIAL INFRASTRUCTURE

DOMAINS
Copper/aluminum conductors, electrical steel, magnets, power semiconductors, batteries/storage, transformers, switchgear, cables, industrial gases, specialty chemicals, cement/steel, construction equipment, and engineering services.

THESIS METHOD
Trace physical intensity per unit of buildout, capacity additions, qualification, geographic concentration, recycling/substitution, and price pass-through. Avoid simplistic “demand up = stock up” logic.

BOTTLENECKS
Mine/permitting timelines, refining, specialty grades, qualified manufacturing, grid equipment, skilled trades, logistics, and environmental constraints.

VALIDATION
Order books, utilization, lead times, contracts, pricing, capacity expansions, permitting, customer qualification, and cash returns on new capacity.

RISK
Commodity cycles, China exposure, substitution, overcapacity, cost inflation, project delays, and capex overruns. Prefer companies with differentiated processing, qualification, or installed service networks over pure price takers.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
