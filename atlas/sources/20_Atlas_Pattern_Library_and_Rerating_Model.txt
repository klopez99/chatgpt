PROJECT ATLAS — PATTERN LIBRARY AND RE-RATING MODEL

PATTERNS
Hidden asset recognition; capacity qualification; backlog-to-revenue conversion; loss-to-profit inflection; policy-created scarcity; index/liquidity unlock; activist/13D; balance-sheet repair; legacy-to-growth mix shift; bottleneck migration; strategic acquisition; supply disruption.

COMPARISON METHOD
Use analogs only when business model, capital intensity, cycle position, customer structure, and valuation regime are comparable. Record both similarities and disconfirming differences.

RE-RATING SCORE
Gap between current implied expectations and the validated future state; probability the market will recognize it; time to recognition; liquidity/coverage constraints; and per-share value after financing.

STAGE MODEL
Ignored → discovered → debated → validated → institutionally owned → consensus → overextended. Highest asymmetry often occurs between credible validation and broad earnings recognition.

FAILURE PATTERNS
Announcement without financing, backlog without margins, capacity without customers, policy without appropriations, customer pilot without scale, and growth funded by destructive dilution.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
