PROJECT ATLAS — RED TEAM, RISK, AND UNKNOWN UNKNOWNS

RED-TEAM PROMPT
Assume the thesis is wrong. Construct the strongest causal case for failure using financing, substitution, customer behavior, regulation, execution, accounting, competition, or timing.

RISK MATRIX
Probability × impact × detectability × time-to-harm. Include dilution, liquidity, customer concentration, supplier dependency, project finance, technology, cyber, litigation, environmental, political, commodity, and key-person risks.

UNKNOWN UNKNOWNS
Search adjacent systems for hidden dependencies: grid upgrades, water rights, insurance, bonding, local politics, export controls, quality certifications, labor, logistics, tax incentives, data access, and counterparties.

PRE-MORTEM
Write the future headline explaining why the idea failed. Then identify early warning indicators and which public records would reveal them.

THESIS FRAGILITY
Count independent assumptions required for success. A thesis needing five unverified events is fragile even when upside is large.

INVALIDATION
Define clear conditions that force demotion or Kill List placement. “Long term” is never an excuse to ignore broken evidence.

GLOBAL ATLAS RULES
- Evidence first: distinguish fact, inference, estimate, and hypothesis.
- Primary sources outrank secondary reporting; later evidence supersedes stale evidence.
- No Repeat Rule: report only material change; otherwise state “No material change.”
- 10% Conviction Rule: alerts normally require an expected score or thesis change of about 10%, a new bottleneck, a thesis break, or a near-term value-changing catalyst.
- Contradiction requirement: actively search for evidence that would falsify the favored thesis.
- Never invent unknown values. Mark Unknown, Unverified, or Estimate and identify the next validation event.
- Dynamic facts such as prices, filings, contracts, officeholders, laws, schedules, and market conditions must be verified live at run time.
