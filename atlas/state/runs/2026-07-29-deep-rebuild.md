# Project Atlas — Deep Rebuild

- Rebuild date: 2026-07-29
- Coverage universe: All 25 Atlas modules; active state plus fresh U.S.-listed infrastructure screen
- Prior rebuild date: Initial full rebuild after Atlas OS deployment
- Overall regime verdict: **MATERIAL CHANGE — power access remains the dominant bottleneck, but the investable layer is migrating toward financed, energized, integrated physical infrastructure.**

## 1. Executive conclusion

Atlas rebuilt the active universe from first principles and found four decision-quality outcomes:

1. **CORZ remains WATCH, Stage 0/1 boundary, Atlas Conviction 82.** AMD’s approximately 530 MW anchor across five sites and the broader 2.5 GW option validate Core Scientific’s AI-infrastructure conversion, while construction, financing, debt and warrant economics still prevent a STARTER verdict.
2. **VRT enters Core as BUY, Stage 3, Atlas Conviction 89.** It is the cleanest validated public expression of the rising thermal-and-power complexity of high-density AI facilities, supported by capacity expansion, NVIDIA-linked reference designs, strong orders, backlog, margins and cash flow.
3. **PWR enters Core as BUY, Stage 3, Atlas Conviction 88.** Record electric-infrastructure backlog and accelerating utility/transmission demand make it a cleaner second-order beneficiary than most speculative power owners.
4. **MARA enters Emerging as WATCH, Stage 0, Atlas Conviction 76.** Its Starwood agreement and acquisitions of Long Ridge and Texas power assets create a credible owned-power and data-center optionality thesis, but bitcoin exposure, debt, capital allocation and execution complexity keep evidence below STARTER quality.

No existing active thesis was killed. Two speculative themes were added to the Kill List: lightly capitalized “AI data-center pivots” without secured power/customer proof, and GridAI’s current financing-led thesis.

## 2. Evidence audit

### New Tier A evidence
- Core Scientific Q2 2026 disclosure: approximately 1.1 GW total leased customer capacity, approximately 437 MW billing by mid-July, and AMD anchor agreements covering approximately 530 MW with more than $14 billion potential base contracted revenue.
- Core Scientific’s $3.3 billion 7.75% senior secured notes due 2031 confirm that the conversion thesis is heavily capitalized but materially leveraged.
- Meta/BlackRock El Paso venture: 1 GW campus, approximately $14 billion total development cost, BlackRock-managed funds owning 80%, and Meta leasing the full campus.
- MARA SEC filings: Starwood strategic agreement for existing U.S. mining sites; approximately $1.5 billion Long Ridge acquisition; Texas power-asset acquisition; Q1 2026 cash, debt, bitcoin holdings and share count.
- Quanta Q1 2026: $48.5 billion total backlog, $26.2 billion remaining performance obligations, and raised 2026 expectations.
- Vertiv primary disclosures: 2025 year-end backlog of $15.0 billion, fourth-quarter organic orders up approximately 252%, 2026 guidance raised after Q1, and manufacturing/test expansion for liquid cooling and integrated power.

### Stale evidence removed
- No reliance on prior narrative rankings that lacked repository state or current source support.
- No credit given to announced capacity unless it is differentiated as controlled, financed, contracted, under construction, billing, or operational.

### Contradictions found
- CORZ: leverage, capex burden, customer acceptance, equipment lead times, and warrant dilution can still impair per-share value despite large contracted-revenue headlines.
- MARA: the power-infrastructure strategy competes with a volatile bitcoin balance sheet, material debt and complex capital allocation.
- VRT: enormous order acceleration creates execution and normalization risk; valuation may already reflect substantial success.
- PWR: strong backlog is partly estimated under MSAs and may not convert with the economics or timing implied by headline totals.

### Claims still unverified
- Exact AMD warrant quantity, exercise mechanics, termination provisions and site-level economics.
- Fully diluted CORZ valuation and project-level free cash flow per MW.
- MARA’s final funding mix and return thresholds for Long Ridge and subsequent data-center conversion.
- Supplier awards for Meta/BlackRock’s El Paso project.

## 8. Red-team findings

- **Strongest case Atlas is wrong:** AI-capex demand may remain real while economics accrue mainly to hyperscalers and financiers; contractors/suppliers could face capacity normalization, customer bargaining power, labor inflation and delayed projects.
- **Most fragile thesis:** MARA. Success requires multiple independent events—asset closings, financing, conversion, customer leasing and bitcoin-risk management.
- **Hidden dependency:** Large AI campuses require synchronized utility upgrades, transformers, switchgear, cooling systems, financing, permitting and customer acceptance. Failure at one node can defer revenue for years.
- **Pre-mortem headline:** “AI infrastructure developers overbuilt power positions, financed at peak expectations, and suffered multi-year delays as utilities, equipment and customers failed to synchronize.”

- Foundation exposure: VRT and PWR fit the highest-quality physical-infrastructure bucket, subject to valuation discipline.
